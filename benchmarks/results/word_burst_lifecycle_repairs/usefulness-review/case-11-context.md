# A row of labels nobody reads

Used permanent marker on pale wooden sticks; after rain the writing is almost invisible. Neatness at planting did not survive the conditions of use. Photograph the bed from the path before replacing them. [[Garden notebook]]: the label is part of the experiment, because losing its identity makes every later observation harder to interpret.

# Garden notebook

The allotment has a sunny line of pots, two beds beside the wall and a low patch near the shed. The thermometer lives in the shed and reads warmer than the exposed bed. Record the position of a measurement before comparing it with another. A neat number without its location is not much of an observation.

This year I want to keep records of failed seedlings as well as the survivors. Save seed in labelled envelopes and photograph the beds from the path so an untidy label does not erase their identities. Watering arrangements need named places, not a vague promise to do the garden. [[Field methods]] may help me keep the small experiments honest without turning a Saturday outside into a research bureaucracy.

# Exhibition plan

The harbour archive opened and the project is complete. The registrar owns recordings, permissions and later withdrawal requests. A speaker can request removal through the museum contact address; the duty curator can remove an extract while a longer review takes place. Keep the permission table with the recording, not just beside a panel layout that may later change.

This page is now an archive of the project. Do not copy its completed tasks into next month's plan. The useful unfinished responsibility belongs to named people in the museum, not to the original volunteer group. [[Listening]] records the approach to speakers and [[Field methods]] records selection limits. The project's smaller presence in my working notes is the intended consequence of a successful handover.

# When a project is allowed to end

Closing the harbour archive required more decisions than opening it. Someone must own withdrawal requests after the volunteers leave. Completion is a transfer of obligations, not their disappearance. Write a small handover receipt with named people and dates. [[Maintenance]] might belong in the project brief at the beginning, while there is still a budget to argue about.
