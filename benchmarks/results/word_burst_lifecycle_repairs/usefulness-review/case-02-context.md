# Maintenance

Maintenance begins with noticing a small failure before it makes an object unusable. I want a shelf for tools, labels for spare parts and photographs taken before dismantling anything. A repair log should identify the fault, the intervention and what happened when the object returned to use. Without that last step I may remember a successful afternoon rather than a lasting repair.

The work is partly material: threads, switches, cracked clay and corroded contacts. It is also a decision about how long to keep an object. A repair that demands specialist equipment every month may be less sensible than retiring something safely. Keep the old part until the replacement has actually worked. [[Pottery]] provides examples where the failure remains useful even when the object cannot return to its original job.

# A seed order is a weather forecast

Ordered broad beans because last winter's surviving plants looked convincing. One successful row does not tell me how many seedlings the slugs ate before I started counting. Keep the failed pots in the notebook this year. The shed thermometer reads two degrees warmer than the exposed bed. [[Garden notebook]] needs the location of each measurement, otherwise the numbers will look more comparable than they are.

# A bicycle light that cannot be repaired

Opened the old rear light and found the battery soldered under a plastic tab. The circuit still works; the case will not survive another opening. It feels peculiar to call the whole object worn out when one small cell has failed. Kept the screws in a labelled envelope. Perhaps [[Maintenance]] should distinguish replaceable consumables from a product that makes ordinary replacement destructive.
