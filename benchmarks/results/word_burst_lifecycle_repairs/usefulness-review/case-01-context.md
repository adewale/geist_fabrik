# The harbour archive consent table

Three speakers returned the harbour archive consent forms with different sections crossed out. A single yes-or-no field cannot represent these choices. One permits the transcript but not her voice; another wants his surname removed. Keep permission attached to each extract rather than to the interview as a whole. The database can wait until I understand the paper table. [[Listening]] and [[Field methods]] both need this example.

# Listening

Listening is not the same as producing a clean record. An interviewer can remove hesitation and accidentally remove a person's uncertainty. For the harbour archive I want to preserve what the speaker is prepared to share, including the right to leave a question unanswered. We need a record of permission for each public extract, not just a signature for the whole conversation.

The practical test is whether a speaker recognizes the resulting account as something they chose to say. Returning a transcript is only one method. Playing a short recording together may reveal what a corrected sentence cannot. Do not treat disagreement with my summary as a defect to repair in the speaker. See [[Field methods]].

# Whose voice survives a transcript

The harbour archive starts with six interviews and a consent form designed for photographs. A recording contains a neighbour's cough, a kettle and an unplanned argument; a transcript makes these people sound like prepared witnesses. I want to keep a short ambient extract beside the cleaned text, but permission to record isn't permission to publish. Ask each speaker what they want removed before asking the curator what fits the exhibition. [[Listening]] might need a practical rule rather than another definition.

# Pottery

Working notes from the Tuesday class. Clay remembers uneven thickness during drying and firing; my hands do not reliably notice it while throwing. Photograph the section of a broken pot before sweeping it away. Record glaze combinations on the base of a test tile and keep the tile beside the recipe.

I prefer handles that look spare, but a handle is also a thing somebody must grip while the cup is hot and heavy. Test with water and with different hands. The class cupboard makes it possible to work with very few personal tools. Do not confuse that shared support with proof that nobody needs equipment. [[Maintenance]] holds the repair and failure notes. This page should remain a place to return to making, not a list of objects I owe the world.

# Field methods

Write down the situation before explaining it. Who was present, where did I stand, what was asked, and who could not take part? Separate a direct observation from a person's account and from my interpretation. A notebook can preserve all three without pretending they have equal certainty.

Useful comparisons name the denominator and the conditions. A photograph of a busy table does not establish how often the table is busy. An interview selected through friends does not represent everybody who lives nearby. Keep exclusions visible and preserve the cases that did not fit the first story. [[Listening]] is a companion practice: leave room for the person to disagree with the account. Small practical decisions still need evidence, but they do not all need a grand claim about the world.
